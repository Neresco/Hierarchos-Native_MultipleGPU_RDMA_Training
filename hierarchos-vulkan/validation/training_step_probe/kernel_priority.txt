kernel priority list, rebuilt from the stride-17 (unperturbed) profile
=====================================================================

Provenance. Every number below comes from the per-shader profile of the canonical
synthetic step (training_step_probe, tokens 8, sequences 1, synthetic token cycle,
warmup 1) with the complete shader table (HIERARCHOS_VULKAN_PROFILE_TOP_SHADERS=4096)
and the default sampling stride 17. Source logs and the generated tables:

  target/prof_check/rank_b2.log    -> kernel_ranking_b2.txt     (batch 2,  5 steps)
  target/prof_check/rank_b32.log   -> kernel_ranking_b32.txt    (batch 32, 5 steps)
  target/prof_check/rank_b512.log  -> kernel_ranking_b512.txt   (batch 512, 5 steps)
  target/prof_check/rank_s5_b32.log-> kernel_ranking_s5_b32.txt (batch 32, 8 steps, stride 5)
  target/prof_check/rank_s1_b32.log-> kernel_ranking_s1_b32.txt (batch 32, 3 steps, stride 1)
  target/prof_check/rank_s1_b512.log->kernel_ranking_s1_b512.txt(batch 512, 3 steps, stride 1)

kernel_ranking.txt is the batch-512 stride-17 table (regenerated).

---------------------------

  Every column is the stride-17 table. The exact-bracket (stride 1) run at batch 512
  differs by less than half a point on the leaders (rwkv.recurrent 77.89%, layer-norm
  7.44%, elementwise-reduction 4.65%, projection.linear 4.79%), because at that batch
  size the instrument barely moves the span.

GPU step total: 61.716 ms at batch 2, 85.054 ms at batch 32, 1284.871 ms at batch 512.
Wall step (probe median): 78.279 ms, 124.148 ms, 1550.024 ms.

So "the" priority list does not exist: at the trainer's default batch the layer-norm /
low-rank-producer family leads, and at batch 512 the packed recurrent kernels lead.
The stride-1 tables give the same order in both regimes, so the order is not a
sampling artifact; only the levels move (see "What stride 1 changes" below).

  batch 2:   8 shaders,  3124 of 6912 dispatches (45.2%),  7.570 ms of  61.716 (12.27%)
  batch 32:  9 shaders,  3432 of 7056 dispatches (48.6%), 10.523 ms of  85.054 (12.37%)
  batch 512: 3 shaders,  1538 of 7120 dispatches (21.6%),  6.245 ms of 1284.871 (0.49%)

  Their recording is the other half, at 1.5-3.6 us per recorded dispatch (wall minus
  span: 10.5 ms / 6912 at batch 2, 25.5 ms / 7056 at batch 32): 4.7-11.2 ms at batch 2,
  5.1-12.4 ms at batch 32, 2.3-5.5 ms at batch 512. So the class is 6-14% of a 78 ms
  batch-2 step and 5-13% of a 124 ms batch-32 step, and under 1% of the 1550 ms
  batch-512 step, where only three of the nine kernels still qualify at <= 6 us each.

  Execution alone would not pay for restructuring them: 12% of GPU time at the small
  batches. Their recording cost is what makes the class worth attacking, and it is the
  same order as their execution. The eighth pass's size estimate (~10 us per recorded
  dispatch) and this list's (1.5-3.6) agree on the mechanism and disagree on the level,
  because the earlier number came from a run whose own instrumentation inflated it; the
  harness in validation/placement_probe/ measures the per-dispatch host cost directly
  as wall minus GPU at 1.7-5.4 us for these shapes.

  What would have to change first: the per-slot pattern. gradient_accumulate4 is one
  shader at 768 call sites, so it is the cleanest single candidate; the 1346
  optimizer.gradient-bookkeeping dispatches remain blocked on a gradient slab, because
  each optimizer slot owns its own allocation and storage bindings cannot be indexed
  without descriptor indexing.

What stride 1 changes, and which numbers this list uses
-------------------------------------------------------

Stride 17 is the ranking's basis because it is the unperturbed one: stride 1 writes two
timestamps per dispatch, and those writes serialize the compute pipeline, so every
bracket measures an *isolated* dispatch. At batch 32 the same step is 110.384 ms of
brackets at stride 1 against 86.431 ms at stride 5 and 85.054 ms at stride 17, while the
wall clock goes the other way (204.220 ms at stride 1 against 124.148 ms at stride 17).

  kernel                                                       stride 17   stride 5   stride 1
  layer_norm_low_rank_producer_forward_fused    (us/disp)         107.4      105.1      134.5
  packed_cell_channel_mix_state_forward_fused   (us/disp)          64.9       64.8       81.4
  rwkv_time_mix3_..._packed_fast                (us/disp)          75.7       72.7      108.6
  layer_norm_adapter_forward_fused_512          (us/disp)          44.7       44.3       58.7

  Strides 5 and 17 agree within 4%; stride 1 is 20-30% above both. So the order is
  stable and the level in this list is the stride-17 one, with the caveat that even it
  is an upper bound: 1 in 17 dispatches is still isolated by its own timestamps.

  At batch 512 the instrument does not move the span at all (1341.762 ms at stride 17
  against 1341.146 ms at stride 1), because the step is long enough that the isolated
  dispatches still fit; at batch 32 the span moves 98.646 -> 130.311 ms. The
  instrument's distortion is worst exactly where the per-dispatch cost is smallest.

  Sampling also distorts the placement table more than the cost table, and there it is
  not a matter of a few percent:

  * Rows disappear. layer_norm_adapter_forward_fused_512's 3-sample cold/warm pair at
    batch 512 straddles a row that the stride-1 run has 96/48 samples for, and
    packed_state_slot_backward (8/8 at stride 1) has no row at all at stride 17,
    because a shape whose sampled dispatches all landed in one population is filtered
    out. A placement table read at stride 17 is a table of the shapes that happened to
    be sampled, not of the shapes the step ran.
  * Means move by up to 40x. At batch 512 the producer reads +0.464 us at stride 17
    (3 cold, 6 warm) against +20.196 us at stride 1 (48 cold, 96 warm).
  * At batch 32 the same comparison is much better behaved: producer +27.269 (3/6)
    against +29.921 (48/96), bias_grad +8.273 (9/5) against +10.107 (96/56),
    vector_add +0.997 (11/6) against +1.055 (140/120), finite_clamp_forward +0.303
    (7/4) against +0.287 (144/80).

  So: cost ranking from stride 17, placement ratios from stride 1, and every placement
  number in this list is quoted with its sample counts.

What the assigned-placement harness adds
----------------------------------------

validation/placement_probe/ (src/bin/placement_probe.rs) dispatches one kernel both ways
with placement *assigned*: an explicit barrier before every cold dispatch, none before
the warm ones, both arms alternating in both orders, barrier counts read back from the
command stream before a block is timed, and identical buffers in both arms so no
aliasing confound is introduced. Per dispatch, medians over 8 alternating blocks:

  kernel / shape                       grid      warm us   cold us   inherited
  linear_forward      32x128x128       8x2x1       4.34      15.96      +11.6
  linear_bias_forward 32x128x128       8x2x1       4.34      16.33      +12.0
  linear_bias_forward 512x128x16       1x32x1      8.49      19.29      +10.8
  vector_add          1x65536x1        256x1x1     3.75       6.03       +2.3
  finite_clamp_forward 1x65536x1       256x1x1     3.24       3.80       +0.6
  linear_forward      512x128x128      8x32x1     30.06      34.12       +4.1
  linear_bias_forward 32x512x512       32x2x1     20.53      50.27      +29.7

  Three things the profile's observed split cannot show:

  1. Inheritance is a property of the shape class, not a constant. A single elementwise
     stream over 256 workgroups pays ~0.6 us for the barrier in front of it; a narrow
     matmul shape pays ~11-12 us; the widest shape measured pays ~30 us.
  2. For compute-bound wide shapes it is also a property of the *block*: the same
     dispatch costs 50.44 us at 32 dispatches per submission and 20.53 us at 128, so
     most of what a barrier costs there is the inter-dispatch overlap it prevents, not
     a fixed flush. A "cost per barrier" constant does not exist.
  3. Identical buffers and identical neighbours in both arms put finite_clamp_forward at
     +0.6 us, while the step's profile reports -4.588 us (144 cold, 80 warm) for the
     same kernel at the same grid. The profile's cold/warm difference therefore carries
     the *neighbour* kernel's identity, not only the kernel's response to placement.

  Limits of the harness: it measures kernels whose dispatch parameters are already
  pinned down by an existing validation harness (the two linear kernels, vector_add,
  finite_clamp_forward), so its numbers transfer to the wide recurrent kernels by
  argument, not by measurement; its GPU reading goes through the device's own
  per-dispatch timestamps, so its absolute levels carry the same isolation as stride 1
  while the cold/warm difference stays comparable; and each block's dispatches write
  distinct output buffers, which the real step does not.

Not established here
--------------------

  * Whether the batch-512 top four can be made faster at all. Their arithmetic is
    required by the step; no measurement in this pass touches their internals.
  * What the producer's 107 us per dispatch is spent on at batch 32. Its own cost is
    bounded (about 20-30 us of it is placement, and ~20-25% of the cold time at stride
    1), but the remaining ~80 us is unattributed: the harness cannot instantiate it.
  * The exact host cost in a real step. wall minus span gives 1.5-3.6 us per recorded
    dispatch, but it also carries per-submission submit and readback costs that the
    synthetic harness does not separate.
